Policy Uncertainty and Institutional ESG Investments: U.S. Mutual Funds Portfolio Rebalancing

SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=7278598

Abstract: The paper shows that U.S. mutual funds systematically increase holdings of high-ESG stocks during periods of elevated policy uncertainty, consistent with using ESG characteristics as hedging instruments against regulatory risk. Interestingly, conventional funds without environmental mandates exhibit stronger hedging responses than dedicated green funds. The effects strengthen during the COVID-19 period and persist across alternative uncertainty measures, suggesting that ESG characteristics provide state-dependent financial benefits beyond their environmental merits.

Institutional Ownership and Biodiversity Risk

SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=7278641

Abstract: This paper examines how institutional investors respond to biodiversity risk and how ownership structures relate to corporate governance. Using US equity data over 2010-2024, I document that institutional investors systematically reduce exposure to biodiversity-sensitive industries, particularly when these risks receive greater media attention. At the firm level, reduced institutional monitoring associates with increased biodiversity disclosure, consistent with a governance gap mechanism. These findings establish biodiversity as a distinct financial risk factor that shapes institutional portfolio decisions and corporate transparency.

ESG Industry and ESG Ratings: Survey, Challenges and Data Analysis

SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=7187438

Abstract: This paper surveys the academic and industry literature on ESG ratings and provides original empirical evidence of rating instability within LSEG (Refinitiv). Comparing two database downloads separated by one year, I document systematic retroactive score revisions, a structural coverage expansion after 2015 that distorts longitudinal comparisons, and sectoral heterogeneity in rating adjustments. Correlation analysis shows that relative rankings deteriorate measurably for recent years, with material implications for ESG-based empirical research and investment practice.

Convergence versus Divergence of Investors' Legal Protection and Corporate Borrowings across Emerging Markets

SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=7283619

Abstract: This paper examines how the balance of legal protections for creditors and shareholders shapes corporate borrowing in emerging markets. I distinguish four institutional configurations - inclusive convergence, dual weakness, creditor-biased divergence, and shareholder-biased divergence - and show that inclusive convergence, where both investor groups receive strong and balanced legal protection, is associated with substantially higher corporate leverage. Creditor-biased divergence correlates with reduced borrowing, consistent with over-liquidation risk discouraging debt financing. These patterns are most pronounced for financially constrained, technology-intensive, and financially distressed firms. The findings suggest that legal reforms strengthening only one dimension of investor protection may be less effective than developing rights for all financial stakeholders.
